# Quality Committee Brief
## Canadian Hospital Readmission Analysis
**Prepared for:** Hospital Quality and Patient Safety Committee
**Data source:** CIHI Quick Stats — Hospital Indicators, 2018–2022
**Date:** 2024

---

## What We Measured
30-day all-cause readmission rates across 10 Canadian provinces and 8 high-burden diagnoses, using CIHI aggregate hospital indicator data. A readmission is counted when a patient returns to any acute care hospital within 30 days of discharge for a related condition.

---

## Three Things This Committee Needs to Know

**1. Atlantic Canada has a persistent readmission problem.**

NL, NS, and NB all exceed the national average of 11.1%. NL sits at 12.7% — the highest in the country. This gap has been present across every year in the dataset. It is not a one-year anomaly. The most likely cause is limited post-discharge primary care capacity, which means patients return to hospital when their condition worsens rather than being managed in the community.

**2. Two diagnoses are driving the majority of preventable readmissions.**

COPD and Heart Failure have the highest readmission rates of the eight conditions tracked. Together with Sepsis, these three diagnoses represent 39% of all readmissions in scope. All three have well-evidenced post-discharge protocols that reduce return rates — structured medication reconciliation, 48-hour follow-up calls, and remote monitoring for Heart Failure patients.

**3. COVID made things worse, but rates have recovered.**

The 2020 disruption added approximately 1.0 percentage point to the national rate — roughly 1,200 additional readmissions that year. By 2022, rates had returned to within 0.2 percentage points of the pre-COVID baseline. The system largely recovered, but Atlantic provinces are recovering more slowly than the national average.

---

## What a 10% Reduction Would Mean

| Scenario | Readmissions Prevented | Estimated Saving |
|----------|----------------------|-----------------|
| 5% reduction — COPD, HF, Sepsis | ~1,494 | ~$13.7M/year |
| 10% reduction — COPD, HF, Sepsis | ~2,988 | ~$27.5M/year |
| 15% reduction — COPD, HF, Sepsis | ~4,482 | ~$41.2M/year |

*Cost per readmission: $9,200 CAD (CIHI estimated average acute care episode cost)*

A 10% reduction is achievable. Published evidence from comparable Canadian programs shows that structured discharge follow-up for COPD and Heart Failure patients reduces 30-day readmissions by 8–15% within 12 months of implementation.

---

## Recommended Actions

| Priority | Action | Province / Scope | Timeline |
|----------|--------|-----------------|----------|
| 1 | Implement 48-hour post-discharge call protocol for COPD and HF | NL, NS, NB first | 3 months |
| 2 | Standardise medication reconciliation at discharge for top-3 diagnoses | National | 6 months |
| 3 | Request CIHI publish readmission data by discharge destination | CIHI advocacy | 12 months |
| 4 | Establish province-level readmission monitoring dashboard | Atlantic provinces | 6 months |

---

## What We Cannot Conclude From This Data

- Whether readmissions are to the same hospital or a different facility
- Whether readmissions are preventable vs. expected clinical deterioration
- How readmission rates differ by patient age, comorbidity, or socioeconomic status

These questions require patient-level data, which is available through CIHI's Discharge Abstract Database under a formal data sharing agreement.
